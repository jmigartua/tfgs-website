2.1 Detailed Structure of the TFG Report

A thoroughly structured TFG report in Physics must contain:

Introduction

  • Motivation and Context: Clearly present the scientific background, significance, and motivation of your study.
  • Research Questions and Objectives: Explicitly state the research questions you seek to address and the specific objectives.
  • Structure Overview: Briefly describe the organization of your document.

Theoretical Framework

  • Literature Review: Critically analyze previous studies, highlighting contributions and limitations.
  • Key Concepts and Theories: Clearly define and contextualize relevant physics theories, models, and equations.

Methodology

  • Experimental Procedures: Provide detailed descriptions of experimental setups, instruments, and materials used.
  • Computational Models: Clearly explain simulations, software (Python, MATLAB, R), algorithms, and mathematical formulations.
  • Data Collection Methods: Describe the methods for data acquisition and management.

Results

  • Presentation of Data: Utilize figures, tables, and graphs effectively to display your findings clearly.
  • Analysis Techniques: Outline methods of analysis (statistical tests, computational methods).

Discussion and Analysis

  • Interpretation: Relate your results to the original research questions, analyzing patterns, anomalies, and implications.
  • Comparison: Critically compare your findings with previous research and theoretical predictions.

Conclusion

  • Summary of Findings: Clearly summarize main outcomes and their relevance to the field of physics.
  • Limitations and Future Work: Discuss methodological constraints and suggest areas for future exploration.

Bibliography

  • IEEE Citation Standard: Consistently format all references according to IEEE guidelines, ensuring accuracy and completeness.

Appendices

  • Supplementary Material: Include additional data, computational code scripts (clearly commented Python or R code), experimental procedures, and detailed mathematical derivations or proofs.

2.2 Academic Writing Best Practices

  • Clarity and Precision: Maintain logical flow and consistency.
  • Technical Writing: Clearly define all specialized terms upon first use; minimize ambiguity.
  • Avoiding Plagiarism: Properly paraphrase and cite sources; use direct quotes sparingly.
  • Proofreading and Editing: Regularly revise and seek peer feedback; consider grammar-check tools like Grammarly or LanguageTool.

Part II – Preparation of the Deliverable (Report)

2.1 Detailed Structure of the TFG Report

An effectively structured TFG report in Physics will clearly guide readers through your research, from introduction to conclusion. Begin your report by presenting the motivation and context, explaining clearly why your study is important within the broader field of physics. Introduce your research questions and specific objectives, articulating precisely what you aim to investigate or achieve. Provide readers with a brief overview of your document’s structure, clarifying what content each section covers.

In the theoretical framework section, perform a thorough and critical literature review, detailing previous studies relevant to your research. Clearly highlight contributions, identify limitations, and articulate how your research aims to address these gaps. Define and contextualize key physics concepts, theories, and models, laying a strong foundation that will guide your reader through the rest of your analysis.

Your methodology should thoroughly document your research approach, whether experimental, theoretical, or computational. If conducting experiments, provide a clear and detailed description of your setups, including instrumentation, calibration, and measurement procedures. For computational research, explain your models clearly, mentioning the specific software utilized (such as Python, MATLAB, or R), algorithms implemented, and mathematical formulations used. Additionally, clearly articulate your data collection methods, describing how you acquired, processed, and managed your data throughout your research.

The results section must clearly and logically present your findings. Use tables, figures, and graphs effectively to summarize and visually illustrate your data. Each visual element should be carefully labeled and accompanied by a descriptive caption that makes it understandable independently from the text. Specify and justify your chosen methods of data analysis, including statistical tests or computational techniques used.

In the discussion and analysis section, interpret your results comprehensively, linking them back explicitly to your research questions and theoretical framework. Explore any identified patterns or anomalies, providing clear explanations and relating your findings to previously established theories and empirical results. Compare and contrast your outcomes critically against prior research, discussing both confirmations and discrepancies.

Conclude your report by succinctly summarizing the main outcomes and emphasizing their relevance and significance within physics. Explicitly discuss the limitations encountered during your research, reflecting honestly on methodological constraints or potential biases. Clearly articulate suggestions for future research, pinpointing areas or questions your project has opened or left unanswered.

Ensure your bibliography adheres strictly to IEEE citation standards, thoroughly checking for completeness and accuracy in referencing all sources. Include relevant supplementary materials in appendices, such as detailed calculations, computational code scripts (clearly commented and structured), raw experimental data, and extended methodological explanations.

2.2 Academic Writing Best Practices

High-quality academic writing in physics demands clarity, precision, and logical coherence. Write clearly, structuring your arguments logically to facilitate comprehension. Avoid unnecessary complexity or ambiguity, defining all specialized terms clearly when first introduced. Adhere strictly to IEEE citation guidelines, ensuring all sources are cited accurately and consistently to acknowledge contributions properly and prevent plagiarism.

Minimize jargon, using precise language appropriate to the level of your intended academic readership. Regularly proofread your manuscript to improve readability, clarity, and precision. Solicit feedback from peers or your supervisor, integrating constructive criticism to enhance the rigor and clarity of your work. Use grammar-check tools like Grammarly or LanguageTool as an additional resource to identify overlooked mistakes or ambiguous phrasing.

2.3 Writing with an IDE

Visual Studio Code (VS Code) is highly recommended for integrating coding and writing efficiently. Begin by installing VS Code from its official website, then customize your setup with essential plugins to optimize productivity. Plugins like Markdown All-in-One, GitLens, LaTeX Workshop, Code Spell Checker, and Prettier help you maintain consistent formatting, clarity, and organization throughout your writing process.

Alternative IDEs like RStudio or Positron can also be beneficial depending on your specific needs, particularly if your project involves extensive statistical analysis or R-based simulations. Briefly explore their strengths and consider choosing an IDE aligned with your project’s specific requirements.

2.4 Quarto Ecosystem

Quarto is an exceptionally powerful academic writing tool that integrates seamlessly Markdown and executable code chunks. Quarto facilitates writing comprehensive and reproducible reports, enabling clear documentation of your analytical processes alongside narrative text. Insert and execute Python, R, or Julia code directly into your reports to dynamically generate results, figures, and analyses, enhancing reproducibility and transparency.

Utilize Quarto’s versatile export functionalities to effortlessly produce professional-quality documents in various formats, such as PDF using customized LaTeX templates, HTML for interactive web content, or dynamic interactive documents. Quarto’s integrated approach greatly simplifies the process of creating clear, reproducible, and visually appealing academic outputs.

2.5 Version Control

Effective use of version control, particularly Git, is essential in managing your TFG project efficiently. Set up a Git repository at the project’s start to manage changes, track progress clearly, and revert to previous versions if necessary. Regularly commit changes with meaningful messages, clearly indicating updates or adjustments made. Manage project variations and experiments using separate branches, merging them back into the main branch after verification and approval.

Collaborate effectively with peers or your supervisor by using GitHub, which facilitates smooth teamwork, conflict resolution, and backup of project versions. Integrating Git within your IDE (like VS Code) further simplifies version management, allowing seamless monitoring of your revisions and collaborative workflows directly from your editing environment.

2.6 Environments and Execution

Ensuring reproducibility and consistency of your computational environment is vital for your TFG’s credibility. Establish isolated virtual environments using Python (venv), Conda, or R (renv) to maintain clear control of software versions and dependencies. Detailed documentation of your computational setup, using files like requirements.txt or environment.yml, is critical for reproducing your analysis across different computing platforms.

Confirm that your computational environment runs reliably across Windows, macOS, and Linux, testing it thoroughly in different systems. Clearly document installation procedures, environment configurations, and dependencies. Proactively managing these aspects assures reviewers or future researchers can replicate your results accurately and reliably.
